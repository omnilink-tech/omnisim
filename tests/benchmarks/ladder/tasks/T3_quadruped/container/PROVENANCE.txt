T3_quadruped -- what is in this container, where it came from, and what it is
not.

CONTENTS
    walker_description/urdf/walker.urdf     one four-legged robot description

    That is the whole container. There is no world, no ground plane, no
    launch script, no controller, no trajectory, no gait, no policy and no
    checkpoint. Building a scene and making the robot move are the capability
    under test.

ORIGIN
    Authored by this benchmark's own maintainers, 2026-08-02. It is not a
    vendor model, it is not derived from one, and it carries no third-party
    licence obligations. Nobody's first-party support for a real robot helps
    on this tier and nobody's absence of it hurts -- which is a deliberate
    trade, and the cost of it is recorded in meta.json under
    container.authored_here rather than hidden: a column that ships an
    excellent model of a REAL quadruped gets no credit for having it.

GEOMETRY
    Primitive only: boxes, cylinders and spheres. Not one mesh, not one
    external reference, not one package URI. capability-ladder-plan.md
    section 9 Q4 names the risk that a column fails a tier on a mesh-decoding
    quirk and the grid reads as a capability verdict; T1's container carries
    COLLADA meshes and lives with that risk on the record, and this tier does
    not compound it.

STRUCTURE
    13 links, 12 revolute joints, no fixed joints anywhere.
      base_link                            0.40 x 0.20 x 0.10 m, 6.0 kg
      hip_{fl,fr,rl,rr}                    abduction about x, +/- 0.70 rad
      thigh_{fl,fr,rl,rr}                  flexion about y, +/- 1.60 rad
      shank_{fl,fr,rl,rr}                  knee about y, -2.50 .. -0.20 rad
    Total declared mass 10.80 kg. Link lengths: thigh 0.20 m, shank 0.20 m,
    hip offsets (+/-0.18, +/-0.09, -0.02) from the base centre.

    The absence of fixed joints is deliberate. An importer that fuses welded
    bodies erases a named foot link, and the tier counts make-and-break ground
    contacts per contacting body -- so each shank carries its own foot sphere
    and stays a named body on every column.

COLLISION
    The base box and the four foot spheres (radius 0.022 m at each shank tip).
    Thighs and shanks are visual. The only bodies on this robot that can
    appear in a contact are therefore base_link and shank_{fl,fr,rl,rr}.

WHAT URDF CANNOT SAY, AND WHAT THAT COSTS HERE
    A URDF has no world, no ground, no light, no actuator and no rotor
    inertia. The first four are ordinary and every column adds them. The
    fifth is not ordinary and it bit hard during this container's own
    achievability check: with no rotor inertia (armature) and an explicit
    integrator at a 2 ms step, a stiff joint-position servo on a 0.00085
    kg.m^2 shank is NUMERICALLY unstable, and the robot cannot even stand --
    told to hold a fixed pose it bounced across the floor at about 1 m/s,
    drifted 1.8 m and flipped over inside 3 s. The same command, with rotor
    inertia added on the scene side, held the base within 0.0001 m for 20 s.
    The full measurement is in meta.json under
    container.authored_here.what_the_demonstration_found_about_THIS_ASSET.

    The URDF root link also has no joint to the world, so an importer that
    welds a jointless body will weld this robot to the ground. That is the
    same finding T1's container already carries and it is not new here.
