T4_humanoid -- what is in this container, where it came from, and what it is
not.

CONTENTS
    strider_description/urdf/strider.urdf   one two-legged robot description
    ARENA.txt                               what the world has to offer

    That is the whole container. There is no world, no ground plane, no
    launch script, no controller, no trajectory, no gait, no policy and no
    checkpoint. Building a scene and making the robot walk are the capability
    under test.

ORIGIN
    Authored by this benchmark's own maintainers, 2026-08-02. It is not a
    vendor model, it is not derived from one, and it carries no third-party
    licence obligations. Nobody's first-party support for a real humanoid
    helps on this tier and nobody's absence of it hurts -- which is a
    deliberate trade, and the cost of it is recorded in meta.json under
    container.authored_here rather than hidden: a column that ships an
    excellent model of a REAL humanoid gets no credit for having it.

GEOMETRY
    Primitive only: boxes and cylinders. Not one mesh, not one external
    reference, not one package URI. capability-ladder-plan.md section 9 Q4
    names the risk that a column fails a tier on a mesh-decoding quirk and the
    grid reads as a capability verdict; T1's container carries COLLADA meshes
    and lives with that risk on the record, and this tier does not compound
    it.

STRUCTURE
    13 links, 12 revolute joints, no fixed joints anywhere.
      base_link              pelvis and trunk in one body, 12.0 kg,
                             0.20 x 0.28 x 0.44 m centred 0.20 m above the
                             hip plane
      hip_yaw_{l,r}          about z, +/- 0.70 rad
      hip_roll_{l,r}         about x, +/- 0.45 rad
      thigh_{l,r}            hip pitch about y, -2.00 .. 1.00 rad, 0.28 m
      shank_{l,r}            knee about y, -2.30 .. -0.05 rad, 0.28 m
      ankle_{l,r}            ankle pitch about y, +/- 0.90 rad
      foot_{l,r}             ankle roll about x, +/- 0.45 rad,
                             0.20 x 0.09 x 0.04 m box
    Total declared mass 25.60 kg. Hip offsets (0, +/-0.085, -0.03) from the
    base origin.

    There are no arms, and the trunk is part of base_link rather than a body
    on a waist joint. Both are deliberate: the tier is a statement about legs,
    every extra body is one more thing a column's importer can differ on, and
    a waist joint would need a controller of its own just to keep the trunk
    up.

    The absence of fixed joints is deliberate too. An importer that fuses
    welded bodies erases a named foot link, and the tier counts make-and-break
    ground contacts per contacting body -- so each foot hangs off its own
    ankle-roll joint and stays a named body on every column.

    The knee's upper limit is -0.05 rad rather than 0. A knee allowed to lock
    straight sits exactly on the singularity of every two-link leg solution,
    where the answer is numerically fine and physically rigid.

COLLISION
    The base box and the two foot boxes. Thighs, shanks and joint housings are
    visual. The only bodies on this robot that can appear in a contact are
    therefore base_link, foot_l and foot_r.

STANDING GEOMETRY (a fact about the file, not a recommended pose)
    With every joint at zero the legs are straight and the sole sits 0.725 m
    below the base origin. With a crouch of hip pitch 0.30, knee -0.60, ankle
    pitch 0.30 the sole sits 0.680 m below it and each foot is still directly
    under its own hip, because the shank rotates back through exactly the
    angle the thigh rotated forward. Each leg reaches between 0.09 m and
    0.60 m from the hip pitch axis to the sole.

WHAT A ROBOT DESCRIPTION CANNOT SAY, AND WHAT THAT COSTS HERE
    This format has no world, no ground, no light, no actuator and no rotor
    inertia. The first four are ordinary and every column adds them. The fifth
    is not ordinary, and on the four-legged tier it bit hard enough to be
    worth repeating here: with no rotor inertia (armature) and an explicit
    integrator at a small step, a stiff joint-position servo on a light shank
    is NUMERICALLY unstable, and the robot cannot even stand -- told to hold a
    fixed pose it bounced across the floor and flipped over within seconds.
    The same command, with rotor inertia added on the scene side, held it
    still. A two-legged robot has less margin than a four-legged one, not
    more, so an agent that reads that first behaviour as "the robot is badly
    modelled" or "the physics is wrong" will fix the wrong thing.

    The root link also has no joint to the world, so an importer that welds a
    jointless body will weld this robot to the ground. That is the same
    finding the tiers below already carry and it is not new here.
