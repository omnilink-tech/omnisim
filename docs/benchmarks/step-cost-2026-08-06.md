**One sentence (as measured 2026-08-06, both backends live):** on single-world
simulation Newton is slower than ODE at every scene size measured, the gap does
not close as the scene grows, and the reason is no longer glue — at 200 bodies
MuJoCo's `mj_step` **alone** costs more than ODE's entire physics phase.

Instrument: [`tests/benchmarks/omnibench/step_cost/`](../../tests/benchmarks/omnibench/step_cost/)
(committed and rerunnable — the point of the exercise). Machine: RTX 3060
laptop, `env_fingerprint` identity recorded in the JSON output. Engine binary
built from `61bc0f14`.

## Why this was measured

Three optimisation commits (`899eb425`, `7b3762f7`, and the `a6aa9e54` revert)
were steered by *"Newton ~0.74 ms/step against ODE's 0.12 ms/step on the same
scene"*. **No script in the tree produced either number.** The first run of one
that does says the target was misplaced in three separate ways.

## Method, and the trap it exists to avoid

`--log-performance=<csv>,<N>` logs the engine's own phase timers for exactly
the first `N` basic timesteps. The same world, run twice with two different
`N`, gives two nested windows; differencing them cancels **every** one-off cost
without having to know what it is.

That matters more here than it usually would. **Newton's `finalizeWorld()` runs
inside the physics bracket on the first tick and measures 1.9–4.9 s.** Over a
1200-step run that is 1.6–4.1 ms/step of pure amortised startup smeared across
the average. A single-window average reports ~2.7 ms/step for a tick that
actually costs ~1.0. Every short-run Newton per-step figure in this repo is
inflated by that term, and it is the most likely explanation for the disputed
0.12/0.74 pair.

`prePhysics + physics + postPhysics` is the reported total. The **controller**
column is deliberately excluded: it is the controller IPC round trip, identical
on both backends, and folding it in is what makes an ODE tick look like 0.12 ms
when its physics phase costs 0.025.

## Result 1 — the sweep

N boxes resting on a static floor, dt 4 ms, median of 3, ms/step:

| bodies | ODE (phys + post = total) | Newton (phys + post = total) | Newton / ODE |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | 0.0112 + 0.0161 = **0.0277** | 0.9360 + 0.2025 = **1.1268** | 40.7× |
| 5 | 0.0246 + 0.0355 = **0.0605** | 0.9903 + 0.3044 = **1.4480** | 23.9× |
| 20 | 0.1060 + 0.1617 = **0.2688** | 1.0786 + 0.9319 = **2.0452** | 7.6× |
| 50 | 0.4594 + 1.0283 = **1.4930** | 1.1846 + 1.1085 = **2.4883** | 1.7× |
| 100 | 0.6932 + 1.4225 = **2.1204** | 2.4056 + 2.4225 = **4.6862** | 2.2× |
| 200 | 1.6803 + 3.1400 = **4.8286** | 4.0215 + 4.5893 = **8.6210** | 1.8× |

**The 5-box row settles the disputed baseline.** ODE's whole tick there is
**0.060 ms**, and its physics phase alone is **0.025** — not 0.12. The 0.12 was
the lane-1 recorder's whole-tick-with-IPC number. The gap being optimised
against was understated by roughly 3.5×: it was **20.7×**, not 6×.

**There is no crossover, and there is not going to be one.** Fitting the sweep,
ODE's marginal cost is ~0.024 ms/body and Newton's ~0.038 — *Newton's per-body
slope is higher*. The curves converge to ~1.6× and never meet. The
"Newton pays a fixed overhead that amortises away at scale" theory is dead.

## Result 2 — the composition flips, and the floor is already too high

`OMNISIM_NEWTON_STEP_PROFILE=1`, same worlds, inside `World.step()`:

| | 5 bodies | 200 bodies |
|---|---:|---:|
| `mj_step` (the actual physics) | 0.072 ms (4%) | **2.094 ms (47%)** |
| newton's `_update_newton_state` (FK re-derivation) | 0.807 ms (48%) | 0.744 ms (17%) |
| OmniSim's own per-tick Python | 0.459 ms (27%) | 0.947 ms (21%) |
| C++→Python crossing | 0.007 ms | 0.011 ms |

**At 200 bodies `mj_step` alone (2.09 ms) exceeds ODE's entire physics phase
(1.68 ms).** A perfect native tick — zero Python, zero glue, zero FK — would
still lose by ~1.25×.

This refutes a claim made in `899eb425` and repeated since: *"bare MuJoCo CPU
is 8–22× faster than our ODE path."* Measured same-machine, same-scene, same
instrument, it is **1.25× slower**. That claim rested on the same 0.12 ms.

The honest reading is that MuJoCo's CPU solver is doing a more expensive and
more exact convex solve than ODE's quickstep. That would be a fair trade if it
bought accuracy — but lane 1 says Newton currently wins only **4 of 11**
correctness metrics, so today we pay MuJoCo's price without collecting
MuJoCo's accuracy.

> ⚠ **Both halves of that trade argument have since weakened.** The "4 of 11"
> figure was heavily qualified by
> [`lane1-validity-2026-08-07.md`](lane1-validity-2026-08-07.md) — most of the gap
> was modelling difference, not error — and it is now **unrepeatable**, because
> lane 1's second in-engine arm *was* ODE (`bdc02139`; its oracle, analytic ground
> truth, is untouched — [correctness-scope.md](correctness-scope.md)). And "we pay MuJoCo's price"
> is no longer a choice: there is no cheaper solver to pay less to. The `mj_step`
> cost figures stand; the comparative framing does not. Fixing the *defaults* (elliptic cone, impratio, substeps,
friction routing) is therefore a better-value lane than shaving glue.

## Result 3 — two hypotheses tested and rejected

**The redundant ODE pass is not a cost.** `mCluster->step()` runs
unconditionally in every Newton world, so a fully-Newton scene appears to pay a
second broad+narrow phase. ([`v6-readiness.md`](../developer/v6-readiness.md)
reaches the same structural observation from the other direction — that "ODE"
names both a *solver* and a collision/ray-casting/sensing *kernel* that runs on
every tick of every world. This is the perf half of that answer: **the kernel's
presence is not what makes Newton slow**, so the case for removing it is about
architecture and licensing, not speed.) Four **interleaved** pairs at 200 bodies (interleaved
because a sequential version of the same comparison drifted 5.7 → 9.7 ms/step
across one session and was worthless): paired deltas **−0.6531, −0.1446,
+0.1214, +1.3385 ms/step**, median **−0.0116 (−0.2%)**, signs disagreeing.
Zero, inside the noise. A disabled body is never integrated, so its broadphase
AABB never moves and ODE's near phase re-derives a static configuration
cheaply. Recorded in the code at the temptation point so nobody runs it again.

> ✅ **The keepalive is gone (`bdc02139`) — and this result is the prediction to
> hold it to.** The redundant ODE pass was removed as an unavoidable consequence
> of deleting `src/ode/`, not as an optimisation. This measurement says to expect
> **no detectable per-step win** from that removal. It also stands as the answer
> to the structural observation it cites: the kernel's presence was never what
> made Newton slow, so its removal is an architecture and maintenance result, not
> a performance one.

**The GPU path is not the answer at nworld = 1 either.** `mujoco_warp` on the
same 200-body world, against CPU `mj_step`: **105.3 vs 11.6 ms/step — 9.06×
slower.**

⚠ **n = 1.** This was set up as a 3-pair interleaved A/B and **only the first
pair completed**; it was stopped after the second pair's warp cell ran ~4×
longer than the first without finishing (the laptop's GPU had been under load
for about an hour and was almost certainly throttling). So the *magnitude* is
soft. The *sign* is not: 9× is far outside this bench's worst observed spread
(±28%), and it is the direction the mechanism predicts. Re-run on a cool
machine before quoting a number.

At one world the warp path is launch-bound — roughly 800 tiny kernel launches
per tick — and the CUDA graph that would elide them is unreachable at the
default substep count (`_can_graph` requires an even count; the default is 1).
Graphing it would help a lot, but the XPBD prototype's 5.3× applied to 105 ms
would still leave it behind CPU's 11.6 here.

## What this means

**Single-world per-step is not a race Newton can win, and three independent
measurements now say so.** Newton's advantage is **batching** — K parallel
worlds on the GPU, where ODE scores zero because it cannot do it at all. That
is the claim worth making rigorous, and it is precisely the one OmniBench
lane 1 structurally excludes (batch size 1) and lane 2 never compares against
ODE.

Concretely, in priority order:

1. **Stop optimising the single-world CPU tick as a way to beat ODE.** It
   cannot get there. Keep doing it for the parts that are embarrassing on their
   own terms — the 24–41× at small scene sizes is ~95% removable glue and it
   hurts the harness loop, CI, and RL deploy — but stop scoring it against ODE.
   > **Post-deletion:** the second half is now automatic and the first half is
   > moot. The work still matters, but it needs an **absolute** target rather
   > than a ratio: **1.127 ms/step at 1 body and 1.448 at 5** is the thing to
   > beat, and ~95% of it is glue, not solve (Result 2: `mj_step` is 4% of the
   > 5-body tick). Losing the ODE column removed the yardstick, not the problem.
2. **Attack the 1.9–4.9 s world-finalize.** It is 30× ODE's whole world load
   and it dominates headless runs, harness hot-reload, and CI. Nobody has
   looked at it.
3. **Make the batched claim rigorous** — a lane that compares K-world Newton
   throughput against K sequential ODE runs is the honest form of "Newton is
   faster", and it is the only form that is true.
   > ⚠ **NO LONGER BUILDABLE (`bdc02139`).** There is no ODE arm to run K times.
   > The best remaining substitute is Newton-batched-GPU against Newton-CPU
   > sequential, which measures *the batching*, not *the engine choice* — a
   > weaker claim, and it must be stated as the weaker claim. Anyone quoting
   > "Newton is faster" now has no in-tree baseline behind it; the only honest
   > phrasing left is about scaling across K worlds, with K and the hardware named.
4. **Fix the Newton defaults** (the six red tests sitting in the tree) so the
   solver cost buys the accuracy it should.
5. **Make `mujoco_warp` CUDA-graphable at odd substep counts** — specified but
   deliberately **not shipped here**. `_can_graph`
   ([`OmNewtonBackend.cpp`](../../src/omnisim/physics/OmNewtonBackend.cpp),
   the `_n_substeps % 2 == 0` clause) refuses to graph a MuJoCo tick unless the
   substep count is even, so the state_a/state_b ping-pong returns to its start
   buffer. The `_use_copyback` machinery that already solves this for XPBD
   would lift the restriction in a two-line condition change. Blast radius is
   narrower than it looks: RL deploy runs substeps=8 (even) and already graphs,
   so this only reaches `mujoco_warp` worlds at an **odd** count — including
   the default of 1. It is not shipped because the measurement above says the
   destination is still slower than CPU, and because this repo has just spent
   three commits relearning that an unvalidated perf change labelled "mechanism
   sound, win unproven" should not ship (`a6aa9e54`). Validate it against the
   RL fixtures, or against a warp world where it is the difference between
   usable and not, before flipping anything.

## Caveats

- One machine, one OS, one GPU. Nothing here is cross-machine.
- Medians of 3 (sweep) and single pairs (warp). Run-to-run spread reached
  **±28%** at 200 bodies, and absolute numbers drifted upward ~60% over a
  ~90-minute session as the laptop heated — which is why every A/B above is
  interleaved and why the sequential ones were discarded rather than reported.
- Box-on-floor scenes only. No articulated robots, no meshes, no motors. A
  scene with many joints and few contacts may compose differently.
- `newtonSolver "mujoco"` (CPU) throughout except where `mujoco_warp` is named.
- ⚠ **The ODE column is unreproducible as of `bdc02139`.** The Newton column can
  still be re-measured with the committed instrument; the ratios cannot. Treat
  every `Newton / ODE` figure in this document as a dated historical reading, not
  a property of the engine.
