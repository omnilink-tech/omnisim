# SparkRover Mini: the printed parts, made on the SV08 printer twin

Generated by `spark_rover/make_print.py`; do not hand-edit. Simulation only: these are the twin's numbers (Klipper's plan, the hotend model, the beads PrintedPart laid), not a real SV08's.

**Mode.** `twin` is a full-physics print on the SV08 twin in the engine (servo-driven axes, PrintedPart). `ideal` is `printing/ideal_print.py`, no engine: the same G-code, Klipper plan, hotend and laying rule, with the nozzle exactly where the steppers would put it. It leaves out the axis dynamics (following error, overshoot, servo jitter), so a part reads about 0.02-0.03 mm smaller a side than on the twin. See [Ideal print (no engine)](../../../../../printing/README.md#ideal-print-no-engine). An ideal result is not a twin result.

**Laying rule.** PrintedPart's bead rule was fixed on 2026-11-04 (see the notes). A row marked "before the bead fix (2026-11-04)" was laid by the old rule: its numbers stay for comparison and are not a current result. Each part's current result (`current_plate`, `mode`, `bead_rule` in printed.json) is on the fixed rule: the twin's once its re-run on the fixed engine has finished and been measured, the ideal mode's until then.

## Notes

- **Beads and blobs (the bead fix, 2026-11-04).** No line is laid wider than the nozzle's flat face (1 mm, 2.5 nozzles): what a step pushes out beyond that, and what leaves a nozzle standing still, is a blob where it left the nozzle, pressed under the face into a disc as tall as the gap, welling up around the nozzle only past what the face holds. Blobs count in the outline and footprint as discs of their width. Before the fix a nozzle slower than 20 mm/s counted as standing still; its plastic was laid on the next step's 0.04-0.05 mm chord as one bead up to 7 mm wide, or, past 0.05 mm3, as a dome above the tip that the next step counted as a nozzle crash (every crash on these plates was one). See "Before the bead fix" below.
- **Hanging strands** inside a part are mostly the first skin layer laid over sparse (10 %) infill, which is what a real printer does too; those at the outline are on visible surfaces.
