REMOVED 2026-08-22 — san_giuseppe_bridge_4k_{...}.{hdr,png}, 12 files, ~7.6 MB
================================================================================

Those twelve files were photographic (31,466-188,990 unique colours per face),
carried no metadata of any kind, and had no in-tree origin record. Their
filename stem matches an HDRI published by Poly Haven —
https://polyhaven.com/a/san_giuseppe_bridge, captured by Greg Zaal — and Poly
Haven's licence page states, verbatim (fetched 2026-08-22,
https://polyhaven.com/license):

    "You can use our assets for any purpose, including commercial work."
    "You do not need to give credit or attribution when using them (although
     it is appreciated)."
    "You can redistribute them, share them around, include them when sharing
     your own work, or even in a product you sell."

with the licence identified as CC0 and no carve-outs listed.

THAT DID NOT SETTLE IT, and this is the point worth keeping. A filename is not
a chain of title. Our files were 1024px cube FACES; Poly Haven ships one
equirectangular panorama. Ours were therefore a derived, re-projected,
downscaled re-encode, so no byte or hash comparison could connect them, and
nothing in the derived files carried the original's identity forward. An
identically-named capture from another source, or a same-place capture by
another photographer, would produce the same filename. If they WERE the Poly
Haven asset there was nothing to fix; the risk was that they were not, and that
risk could not be retired from inside this tree.

So they were deleted instead. icon_studio.omniworld now points at the synthetic
set above, which was already sitting unreferenced in this directory as the
obvious drop-in. Verified after the swap: the world loads with 0 errors and the
engine logs no texture diagnostic for any of the twelve faces
(`python -m omnisim run-headless scripts/icon_studio/worlds/icon_studio.omniworld
--until-finalized --fail-on-warning`). The remaining warning in that run is the
`icon_creator` controller exiting without its `objects.json` PROTO inputs, which
is a property of running a GUI dev tool headless and is unrelated to lighting.

This is a developer icon-rendering rig, so the backdrop was a preference and
never a requirement. If a photographic environment is wanted again, download a
CC0 HDRI yourself, re-project it with scripts/image_tools/, and record the
download URL and date here — that record is the thing that was missing.
